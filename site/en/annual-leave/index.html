How to use

  1. Pick your hire date.
  2. Check the reference date. It starts as today; use your last working day if you are leaving.
  3. To estimate leave pay, enter your monthly ordinary wage (base pay and fixed allowances) and unused days. Enter half days as 0.5.
  4. Read this year's leave, total accrued leave, the next grant, and the leave pay.

How it works

Under one year of service: one day for each full month worked, up to 11 days (Labor Standards Act Article 60(2)).

One year or more: 15 days for 80% or better attendance over the year. From three years of service, one day is added for every two years beyond the first, up to 25 days (Article 60(1) and (4)). That is 16 days in years 3 and 4, 17 in years 5 and 6, and 25 from year 21.

Leave arises the day after each period is completed. Someone hired on January 2 gains a day on the 2nd of each month and 15 days the next January 2. If the month has no matching date, such as a hire on the 31st, it arises the day after that month's last day (Civil Act Article 160).

Unused leave pay = unused days × (monthly ordinary wage ÷ 209 × 8).

Example

Someone hired on March 2, 2024 has worked two full years as of November 13, 2026. They gained 11 days in the first year, 15 days on March 2, 2025, and 15 days on March 2, 2026, 41 days in total. The next 16 days arrive on March 2, 2027.

With a monthly ordinary wage of 3,000,000 won, the daily wage is 3,000,000 ÷ 209 × 8 ≈ 114,832 won, and 5 unused days are worth 574,162 won.

Good to know

  • The annual leave rules do not apply to workplaces with four or fewer regular employees, or to people working under 15 hours a week on average.
  • With attendance under 80% in a year, you get one day per full month instead of 15. This calculator assumes 80% or better.
  • Some employers manage leave by calendar year instead of hire date. Check your company rules.
  • Leave expires if unused within a year of being granted.
  • Results are estimates. Your employment contract and company rules decide the actual leave and pay.

Frequently asked questions

How much leave do I get in my first year?

One day for each full month worked, up to 11 days. These days must be used before your first year ends.

Do I get 15 days if I leave after exactly one year?

No. Korea's Supreme Court ruled that the 15 days arise the day after the first year ends, so someone whose one-year contract ends gets at most 11 days. If employment continues even one more day, the 15 days are added, for up to 26.

How is unused leave pay calculated?

Unused days × daily ordinary wage. For a 40-hour week, the daily wage is the monthly ordinary wage ÷ 209 hours × 8 hours. If the employer properly followed the legal procedure to encourage leave use, it may not have to pay.
